    The basics

    Why RAM is the only thing they'll try to sell you

    What really decides the GB in Minecraft — and it isn't the players.

    The modpack weighs more than the people

    Almost everyone sizes by player count and forgets the rest. But a 200-mod pack with 5 friends needs more RAM than vanilla with 40 people. Every mod loads its blocks, entities and recipes into memory, whether there are two of you or twenty.

    View distance

    Every point of view-distance loads another ring of chunks for every player. Going from 10 to 16 can push usage up 35% without touching anything else. If you're tight, drop it to 8: you'll notice it more in performance than in what you see.

    The CPU matters just as much

    Minecraft leans almost entirely on a single core, so one fast core beats eight slow ones. Lots of RAM won't help a slow CPU. Check which processor you get, not just the gigabytes.

    Java or Bedrock

    Bedrock (consoles and mobile) is a lot lighter. If your group mixes PC and consoles, the usual move is Java with Geyser and Floodgate so everyone can join: it adds a bit of load, but it's how you share one world.

    Questions

    Things everyone ends up asking

    The ones I get asked most in comments and on Discord.

    Can I run the server on my own PC and skip all this?

    You can, but there are three catches: your PC has to stay on all the time (turn it off and your friends can't play), you have to open ports on your router, and your home upload speed is usually far worse than a data centre's, so everyone else will lag. Fine for a one-off evening; not for a serious world.

    I'm on PS5, Xbox or Switch. Can I host a server?

    Not on your own: consoles don't let you run a dedicated server or open ports. If your group is split between PC and consoles and you want to play together in the same world with crossplay, renting a server is literally the only option. In Minecraft you do it by running Java with Geyser and Floodgate, so Bedrock players can join too.

    What if I run out of RAM?

    You'll notice right away: stutter when moving, blocks slow to break, mobs teleporting and, at worst, the server restarting by itself. At a host you fix it by upgrading in two clicks, but it's better to size it first and leave some headroom: the RAM you need goes up over time, not down.

    Is paying for more RAM than I need worth it?

    Not much. Past the point where the game runs smoothly, more RAM doesn't add performance — the CPU calls the shots there. What spare RAM gives you is headroom: room for more mods, more people, or letting the world grow without changing plan.
