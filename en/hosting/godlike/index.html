The good

  • The biggest discount in the whole comparison: −75% on your first purchase. 16 GB comes to €12.75 and 2 GB to €1.57, the cheapest first payment in the table at those sizes.
  • You build the plan yourself: gigabytes (2 to 33), slots (5 to 200) and days — 30, 90, 180 or 360. Hardly anyone lets you pick the duration: if the modpack will last you three months, you pay for three months and close it.
  • Some of the best hardware around: latest-gen AMD Ryzen, up to the 9950X3D, and NVMe drives. Minecraft leans on a single core, and that's where it shows.
  • Up to three backups a day, free, restorable from the panel. Exactly what other budget hosts lack.
  • They give you a free 2 GB server for a day to try the panel before paying anything.
  • The panel was rebuilt in 2025: a console that filters errors, scheduled restarts and backups, a file editor, all the Minecraft settings on one screen with no SFTP, role-based permissions and a mod and plugin installer that checks versions. It works well on mobile and through a Discord bot too.
  • For community owners: an in-game store in one click, a rating system to attract players, and several linked servers.
  • Upgrade or downgrade instantly and pay only the difference. Paying upfront brings it down: −10% for 90 days, −15% for 180 and −20% for 360.
  • Layered DDoS protection with Path.net, ten data centres (Europe included) and payment with almost any method, crypto included.

The catches

  • The −75% is for the first month and only on monthly billing (quarterly or yearly the discount is 9-21%): after that, the normal rate (€25.99 for 8 GB). Factor it in if the server will run all year.
  • At the normal rate it's €3.19 per GB, among the most expensive in the table. Paying the year brings it to €2.54, still above average.
  • A young company, from 2021. In 2024 they had a refund complaint that ended up on Reddit; they explain it on their own site, which says something.
  • The free server is for tinkering: one slot, and you have to renew it every three hours.

Verdict

It's the most flexible host in the whole comparison and one of the best on hardware. Where it wins hands down is the seasonal server: a modpack with friends, an event, a project of a few months. You pick the exact days, try it free and get backups and a CPU that doesn't choke. For a year-round server, do the maths with the renewal price: there are cheaper options there, though with weaker hardware.
